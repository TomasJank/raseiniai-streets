## What his screenshots show (Vilniaus g., walking from no. 114 towards the Bažnyčios g. corner the houses stand in the order 114, 112, 110, 108, 106)

**No. 106, c2462** (seen from the street corner). Walls: old grey rough plaster, patchy, not boards. Roof: steep, dark red-brown corrugated sheets, a brick chimney on the ridge near the left end. The long wall with the number plate faces the street and the ridge runs along it. On the left 45 % of that wall a gabled bay stands flush with the wall and rises almost to the ridge: one ordinary window on the ground floor, one smaller window in the gable. Right of the bay a recessed entrance (a dark doorway with a red brick pier beside it), then one wide low window under the eaves. On the right end wall (the gable end towards the side street) a small wooden balcony at attic level. Low green wire-mesh fence along the plot, lawn, rose bushes at the door. Ground-floor windows are about 1.2 m wide in a wall some 11 m long.

**No. 108, most likely c2463** (seen straight on, and again from the crossing; he had marked c2463 good, so change only what is plainly different). One storey, long side to the street, ridge along the street. Walls: light grey horizontal siding. Roof: dark old corrugated asbestos, a brick chimney left of the middle near the ridge. Street wall: two windows only, each about 1.1 m wide, one a quarter of the way in from the left, one a sixth in from the right (white frames; the right one has a white roller shutter down). The gable end towards no. 106 has dark weathered boards in its upper triangle and a window there.

**No. 110, c2464** (seen straight on and from the right). Walls: dark red-brown horizontal siding everywhere; windows with white frames and dark brown surrounds. It is one house with two parts under brown metal tiles:
- the main part, about 60 % of the street front on the right: one and a half storeys, ridge along the street, a steep roof whose eaves sit at the top of the ground floor and whose ridge is about 7.5 m up. Street wall: one window, right of its middle. Its right end is a gable wall with two ground-floor windows and one attic window. A chimney where the two parts meet.
- a two-storey part on the left 40 % of the street front, flush with the main wall, about 5.3 m to its eaves, under its own low hipped roof that stays below the main ridge. Street wall: a window and a white glazed door with one step on the ground floor (door right of the window), one window upstairs.
There is no second gable to the street: what the game drew as a double roof is this two-storey part. A paved drive on the right, a wrought-iron gate between this house and no. 112.

**No. 112, c2465** (seen from two angles). One storey, low, long side to the street, ridge along the street. The house really is in two halves: the right two thirds have pale yellow horizontal boards, white window frames and an old grey asbestos roof; the left third is a newer extension in the same red-brown siding as no. 110 with one dark-framed window; and the roof over the left half (extension included) is new dark graphite metal tile, meeting the grey asbestos about half way along. Street wall: one window in the brown part, one in the yellow part; the right gable end (towards no. 110) is yellow boards with two ground-floor windows and one attic window.

**No. 114, c2466** (seen straight on and from the right). One storey, long, ridge along the street, grey corrugated asbestos roof, two chimneys on the ridge. Walls: salmon pink horizontal boards (the left third fresher pink, the rest weathered orange-pink), white window frames. Left of the middle a large gabled wall dormer rises from the street wall: cream horizontal siding, two windows side by side, about 30 % of the house's length wide, its peak a little above the main ridge. Street wall, ground floor, left to right: two windows, then (under the dormer) a window, a dark red panelled door, a window, then one more window near the right end. The right gable end has salmon boards below and pale grey siding in the upper triangle with one window.

**No. 67, c2022** (a funeral-services shop; seen from the left front and straight on). Walls: white sand-lime brick. The gable end faces the street, so the ridge runs away from the street. Roof: dark grey tiles, a half-hipped gable (the top of the gable is clipped by a small hip), deep eaves. In the gable a wide two-light window with a black sign board on each side. Across the whole street front, below the gable, a shop front in dark brown timber with six white-framed multi-pane windows and a dark door, under its own lean-to roof of the same dark tiles (hipped at both ends). The left side wall has two windows and a dark timber entrance porch with a white-framed window. Paved all round, no fence, no trees in front. Lettering on the real building names the trade; in the game use plain words only if any (for example "GĖLĖS").
